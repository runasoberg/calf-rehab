Calf Rehab Opportunity Tree

An opportunity solution tree in Teresa Torres's format for the Calf Rehab Coach. It maps the outcome to the unmet needs found in the PRD and the Notion research, then to solution ideas and the assumptions to test first.

Owner: RunaSources: PRD v0.1 (2 Oct), Notion research pages (24 Sep to 7 Oct)Status: draft, not reviewed by a physio
Read this before using the tree

Every opportunity here comes from one person's injury (Grade 2 medial gastrocnemius strain, Feb to Apr 2026). The Notion interviews were completed in Sep 2026 as a retrospective, and many answers are hypothetical ("where would you have gone?"). Torres advises against building on hypotheticals, so those nodes are labelled Inference. I could not tell from the pages whether the GP, physio and nutritionist interviews were held with real clinicians or compiled from literature, so I treat them as secondary until you confirm. No external user has been interviewed, so this tree is a starting hypothesis, not a validated map.

How to read it

OutcomeThe measurable change the product should drive. One at a time.
OpportunityA need, pain point or desire, written in the user's voice. Never a solution.
Sub-opportunityA narrower slice of the need. Solve the smallest one that moves the outcome.
Solution ideaA way to meet it, tagged with the PRD requirement (R1 to R12) it maps to. Several per need, so they can be compared.
● Evidencestated in the logs, interviews or cited literature on the Notion pages ◐ Inferencemy reading of the evidence, or an answer to a hypothetical question ○ Assumptionno direct evidence yet; needs a test

Desired outcome

Business outcome (PRD decision 7, partly open)

Recovery earlier than or on time with the clinical projection, if the plan adherence target is met, with no re-injury within a set window.

Adherence target: not definedRe-injury window: not defined
Product outcome I propose (needs your confirmation)

Raise the share of rehab days with a complete check-in, without raising premature phase advances

This is the PRD's adherence metric (daily check-in completion rate) paired with its counter-metric (an advance followed by more pain or a re-injury within 2 weeks). It sits upstream of the business outcome and is something the product can influence directly. Targets are still TBD in the PRD, so I have not invented one.

Opportunities and solutions

Choosing a target opportunity

Torres's advice is to compare opportunities and commit to one at a time. These ratings are my judgement from the evidence above, not scored data. Rows use the four questions I think matter most for a personal tool: how directly it moves the outcome, how strong the evidence is, whether it is already handled today, and the PRD priority.

My recommendation (opinion)

  • Target O4 first, starting with 4.1 (logging takes effort) and 4.3 (scattered tools). It maps straight to the outcome, has the strongest evidence of an unsolved gap, and the PRD names adherence as the real friction.
  • Treat O2 as a guardrail, not a competitor. Red-flag handling is a hard requirement in every solution, so it should not be traded against other opportunities.
  • Take O3 next (3.1 and 3.2), once the physio has reviewed the exit criteria.
  • Defer O1 for v1. The only user already had a same-week assessment. It matters again if a pilot is ever planned.

A gap in the PRD

O6 (holding back on good days) has no requirement. The close-out log shows multiple falls on skis on day 28 and "pushed harder than I should have" on day 44, and says plainly that the high competitive drive is an asset for adherence and a risk factor for return. Today's pain-based rules would not catch it. Worth deciding whether pace and load caps belong in R5.

Assumption tests to run before building

  • Desirability. Which option do I open daily? Score speed to log and fit with the single-place direction, as plan.md already describes. Set the pass bar before testing, so the result cannot bend it.
  • Feasibility. Extraction accuracy on real past entries, then a deliberate try at tricking the red-flag screen.
  • Usability. How often the clarifying questions interrupt versus help.
  • Viability. Whether Notion as source of truth plus a web UI works without hand-moving files. This is the infrastructure spike (milestone 1).

What a test cannot tell me

Every test above uses one person, who is high-adherence and well resourced, so a pass shows the idea works for her and says little about anyone else. If a pilot is ever planned (decision 5), the same tree should be rebuilt from story-based interviews: "tell me about the last time you logged a session", rather than "would you use this?".

Evidence gaps to close next

Weakest nodes

  • 4.4 (falling off after a missed day) and 8.2 (clinician share) are assumptions with no supporting entry in the notes.
  • 1.2 and 2.3 lean on answers to hypothetical questions or on literature, not on lived behaviour.
  • The DVT co-occurrence figure (6 to 20%) is reported through a 2025 narrative review and was not checked against the original papers, as the Notion page itself says.

Ways to firm it up

  • Mine the Gastrocnemius Rehab Tracker for missed days and what preceded them. That turns 4.4 from assumption into evidence.
  • Confirm whether the GP, physio and nutritionist interviews were real, and re-run any that were not.
  • Have the physio friend review O3 and O7 (decision 3), including the 90% symmetry target, which is a working target borrowed from hamstring research.
  • If a pilot is planned, interview two or three recreational athletes after a clinician-confirmed calf strain, using story-based questions.
